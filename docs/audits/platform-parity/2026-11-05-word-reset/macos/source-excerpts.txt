5: --- DESCRIPTION:
6: --- The text the prediction engine completes: a rolling window of what the user
7: --- typed, kept apart from the hotstring matcher's word buffer so the two have
8: --- their own lifetimes. macOS keeps the same text in its `llm_buffer`; Linux
9: --- feeds this object from the hotstring engine's edits. Both drivers decide
10: --- what a navigation or Escape does to it through the same rule, pinned by
11: --- _shared/tests/corpus/llm/context_buffer_vectors.json.
12: ---
13: --- FEATURES & RATIONALE:
14: --- 1. The hotstring buffer forgets a word on many events (a pause, an arrow,
15: ---    a click, a shortcut) because a trigger must never fire against text the
16: ---    caret has left. The AI context has a different question: should the
17: ---    sentence be sent to the model after the caret moved? That is the
18: ---    `llm.generation.reset_on_nav` preference, nothing else.
19: --- 2. Edits mirror the engine's: typed text is appended, a Backspace drops the
20: ---    last codepoint, and an expansion deletes its trigger and appends what
21: ---    was really produced, so the context is the text actually in the field.
22: --- 3. The window is counted in codepoints and cut at a codepoint boundary,
23: ---    wide enough for the largest `llm.generation.context_length` the prompt
24: ---    builder will ever use.
25: --- 4. Strict input: a non-string text or a malformed count is a caller bug and
26: ---    raises, so the context can never silently drift from the field.
